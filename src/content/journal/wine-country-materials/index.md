Wine country spaces work hard. Guests come in from the vineyard and the patio, weekends are packed, and red wine finds its way onto nearly every surface. Even the best design can start to look tired within a season if the materials aren't chosen for this kind of use. Here are some tips for choosing well.

## Floors

Guests walk in from gravel, dirt, and patios, so floors take grit and moisture all day. Choose a surface that is durable, slip resistant, and easy to clean.

- **Porcelain** resists stains and moisture, and a matte or textured finish adds grip. Ask for the slip-resistance rating and confirm what your local code requires, especially where indoors meets the patio.
- **Sealed concrete** suits a rustic, winery feel and handles heavy traffic, but it is cold and hard underfoot and can make the acoustics challenging. Make sure it is sealed well so wine doesn't stain it.
- **Commercial-grade luxury vinyl planks** give a warm, wood-look floor that helps with acoustics.
- **Good entry matting** at the doors catches dust and grit before it reaches the floor.

## Tasting bars and counters

The tasting bar is the most wiped-down and spilled-on surface in the room. It has to handle red wine, citrus, and cleaning products without showing wear.

- **Quartz** is non-porous, so it resists staining and doesn't need sealing.
- **Natural stone** is beautiful, but marble and other soft stones can stain and etch from the acid in wine. If you love the look, ask about sealing and how the surface will age, and be comfortable with a lived-in patina.
- **Stainless steel and zinc** are hygienic and heatproof. They scratch and patina over time, which many owners treat as part of the character.
- **Solid surface** is seamless and repairable, since scratches can be sanded out, and it works well for curved or custom bars.

## Seating

Comfortable guests stay longer, order more, and come back.

- **Commercial-grade performance fabric** is built for this use. Look for a heavy-duty abrasion rating and a stain-resistant, cleanable finish, and choose deeper tones that hide the occasional splash.
- **Fade resistance matters.** Big windows and glass doors let in strong sun, so ask about the fabric's lightfastness rating, or choose outdoor-rated fabrics near the glass and on the patio.
- **Vinyl and faux leather** wipe clean, and modern versions look far better than the shiny diners of the past, especially in a matte or textured finish.
- **Removable or replaceable cushions** let you swap worn seats without redoing the whole banquette.

## Walls and sound

Barrel rooms, tall ceilings, glass, and stone look wonderful, but they are loud, especially during events.

- **Scrubbable paint** is imperative. Many brands now offer scrubbable matte finishes.
- **Commercial-grade wallpaper** can add personality, though it shouldn't be used in messy or steamy areas.
- **Noise** is one of the most common complaints. Acoustic panels, wood slats, textured plaster, curtains, and rugs soften the sound so guests can enjoy the wine and each other's company.
