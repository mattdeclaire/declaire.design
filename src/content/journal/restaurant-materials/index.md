Even the best restaurant design can begin to look worn after six months of use. Stained banquettes and scuffed surfaces can wreck the best design intentions. Here are some quick tips on selecting the right materials.

## Floors

Floors are the hardest material to replace, so getting them right the first time is imperative. You need a surface that is durable, slip resistant, and easy to clean. Here are the best options:

- **Porcelain** resists stains and moisture, and a matte or textured finish adds grip. Ask for the slip-resistance rating and confirm what your local code requires.
- **Sealed concrete** is inexpensive and industrial, but it is cold and hard, and it can make the acoustics challenging.
- **Commercial-grade luxury vinyl planks** make for a warmer feel and help with acoustics.
- **Quarry tile** is a workhorse in a lot of kitchens, and it continues to show its strength.

## Counters

The counter is the most wiped-down surface in the dining room. It has to handle spills, including citrus, wine, and coffee, along with cleaning products, all without showing wear.

- **Quartz** is non-porous, so it resists staining and doesn't need sealing. It's a safe, durable choice for service counters, though hot pans can damage it, so use trivets or heat-safe zones.
- **Stainless steel** is the commercial kitchen standard. It's heatproof, hygienic, and easy to sanitize. It scratches easily, but many owners treat that patina as part of the look.
- **Solid surface** (like Corian) is seamless and repairable, since scratches can be sanded out. It's a good fit for curved or custom counters.

## Seating and banquettes

You want your customers to be comfortable, enjoy their stay, and come back.

- **Commercial-grade performance fabric** is built for this use. Look for a heavy-duty abrasion rating and a cleanable finish.
- **Vinyl and faux leather** wipe clean and handle heavy use. Modern versions look much better than the shiny diners of the past, especially in a matte or textured finish.
- **Real leather** ages well, but it's more expensive and can stain easily from drinks and oily food.
- **Removable or replaceable cushions** let you swap worn seats without redoing the whole banquette.

## Walls and sound

Protecting your walls and managing noise shouldn't be an afterthought.

- **Scrubbable paint** is imperative. A glossier paint is typically easier to clean than a matte finish, but many brands now offer scrubbable matte paint.
- **Wallpaper** comes in commercial grades. It can add a lot of personality at a modest price, though it shouldn't be used in overly messy or steamy areas.
- **Noise** is one of the most common complaints. Hard surfaces like glass, tile, and tall ceilings let sound bounce around the room. Acoustic panels, textured wall surfaces, hanging wood panels, and curtains can dampen the noise so your patrons can enjoy their meal and their companions.
